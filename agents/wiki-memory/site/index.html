Wiki memory

Retrieval re-reads raw fragments of your documents for every question. A wiki agent does the reading once: it files each new source into pages about the people, systems and policies it mentions, rewrites what changed, and keeps the history.

Skip to the face-off

Not a knowledge graph. A knowledge graph stores typed facts that code traverses. A wiki stores pages of prose that the agent reads and rewrites; the links between them are just references.

    Notes

    What you just read

    The pattern comes from Andrej Karpathy's “LLM Wiki” (April 2026). Raw sources are never edited, the wiki belongs to the model, and a schema file tells it how to keep the wiki. Three operations keep it going: ingest, query and lint.

    The five sources, the pages the agent writes, the captions and the example answers were written by hand to show what a careful model should produce; no model was called. Everything derived from those pages is computed by tested code: the tracked changes, the links, the index and log, the lint findings, the BM25 rankings and the fact checks.

    The face-off is one small example, with raw chunks, three questions and a lexical ranker. It shows the mechanism; it is not a benchmark. The article covers the trade-offs and when retrieval is the better choice.
