How Giskard tests an agent against your own rules

Legal or clinical staff write down a risk the agent must never take. Giskard turns that rule into an attacker, an LLM that plays a user and tries to make the agent break it. Then a judge reads the conversation and decides whether it did. Pick a risk, watch the attacks, and read every verdict. Read the article →

Input

What is prohibited, why, what to do instead, and real user questions. Written by legal or clinical staff.
Giskard

Experts review the verdicts and fix the prompt, the attacks, the judge or the rule.

The attacker and the judge both read the same rule text, so nobody has to translate the policy twice.

The judge can be wrong

Every verdict here comes from an LLM. Before you trust a pass rate, have the rule's owner label a sample of verdicts and measure how often they agree with the judge.

A score depends on the attacker

“9 of 10” means 9 of these 10 generated attacks, each at most 3 turns long. A more persistent attacker, or real users, may find more failures. Ten attacks is a small sample.

Evals don't replace runtime controls

Evals find failures before release. In production you still need guardrails, authorization in code, and human review where the stakes are high.
